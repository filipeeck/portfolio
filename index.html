Philosophy

Design as a strategic function.

I treat design as a tool for shipping the right product — not just a polished one. Six principles I keep coming back to.

  1. 01

    Outcomes over output

    Every decision anchored to a measurable goal — adoption, retention, conversion, efficiency.

  2. 02

    User needs, business reality

    I design with both in view. The best solutions move metrics because they serve users well.

  3. 03

    Pragmatism over perfection

    Ship, learn, iterate. The right answer rarely arrives in a static frame.

  4. 04

    Systems before screens

    Components, patterns, and tokens that scale across teams — not flows built in isolation.

  5. 05

    Decisions under constraint

    Time, budget, technical reality — not limitations, but the brief.

  6. 06

    Collaboration as default

    The best work happens close to PMs and engineers, not behind a wall.

How I work

Where I spend my craft.

Six modes I move between on every project — adjusted to the team, the stage, and the constraints in front of me.

Fast iteration cycles

Short loops between problem, prototype, and feedback. Velocity is a design choice.

Cross-functional partnership

Embedded with PMs and engineers from problem definition through ship — not handed off.

Data-informed decisions

Analytics, usability data, and qualitative signal — feeding judgment, not replacing it.

Scalable design systems

Components, tokens, and patterns treated as a product — versioned, documented, maintained.

Product strategy

Framing the problem, sizing the bet, and aligning teams on what's worth designing in the first place.

Rapid learning across domains

Comfortable in unfamiliar territory. I get fluent fast and bring structure to ambiguous problems.

Modern workflows

Built for how products are shipped now.

Tools, expectations, and team shapes have shifted. My workflow has shifted with them — pragmatically, not as a trend.

Workflow as a product

I evaluate my own tooling the way I evaluate a feature — what compounds, what slows me down, what to deprecate.

AI as an accelerator, not a shortcut

Used for exploration, synthesis, and validation — to compress cycles, not to skip judgment.

Closer to engineering

Lightweight prototyping and code-aware design shrink the gap between concept and shipped surface.

Comfortable with change

The stack will keep moving. Principles, taste, and a real understanding of users are what stay durable.
